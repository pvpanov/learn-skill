You are about to fit a ridge regression on a table where one column is income in euros and another is age in years, and you have to decide whether to standardise the columns first. The choice changes which features the model shrinks towards zero, so it changes the conclusions you would draw from the fit.

Take a concrete case. Suppose both features matter equally: one standard deviation of either moves the target by 2 units. Income has a standard deviation of 20,000 euros and age a standard deviation of 10 years. Then the coefficient on raw income is 2 / 20,000 = 0.0001 per euro, and the coefficient on raw age is 2 / 10 = 0.2 per year.

Ridge regression picks the coefficients that minimise the squared prediction error plus a penalty on the size of the coefficients, with a weight \(\lambda\) that sets how much the penalty counts:

\[ \hat\beta = \arg\min_\beta \sum_i (y_i - x_i^\top \beta)^2 + \lambda \sum_j \beta_j^2 \]

The penalty charges each coefficient by its square, and it has no idea what units the column was measured in. In the example, the income coefficient costs 0.0001 squared, which is 0.00000001, while the age coefficient costs 0.2 squared, which is 0.04: the penalty on age is four million times larger, although both features matter equally. Ridge will therefore shrink the age coefficient hard and leave income almost untouched, and the only reason is that age has a small spread in its own units, which makes its raw coefficient large. Record age in days instead of years and its standard deviation becomes 3,650 days, its coefficient 2 / 3,650, about 0.00055, and its penalty about 0.0000003; the model's verdict on age would change with a choice of units that says nothing about the data.

Standardising each column to mean zero and standard deviation one removes this dependence on units. After standardising, both coefficients in the example equal 2, both pay a penalty of 4 times \(\lambda\), and the shrinkage treats them alike, which is what you want when you have no prior reason to believe one feature matters more than the other. The takeaway for your defaults: standardise the numeric columns before ridge or lasso, using the means and standard deviations of the training split only, and convert the coefficients back to raw units only when you report them.
